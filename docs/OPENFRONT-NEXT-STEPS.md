# Openfront E-commerce: next-step requirements

## Priorities

- **P0:** immediate credential or customer-safety problem.
- **P1:** required before releasing an affected existing journey.
- **P2:** optional product expansion or evidence-led investigation; elevate when an existing merchant commitment depends on it.
- Preserve working customer and merchant outcomes, public integration contracts, historical records and existing safeguards. An inherited defect is not an outcome to preserve. Optional features require an explicit support decision, not merely an available page.

## Confirmed problems

These are current source/contract problems, not claims of deployed incidents.

| ID / priority / user-visible goal | Problem | Acceptance criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **D1 · P0 — My assistant acts only as me** | Assistant requests can send one caller's session to unrelated services, substitute that session for an overlapping second caller, and leave contamination after cleanup. Earlier remediation claims do not match the current behavior. | Overlapping callers, session rotation, setup/stream failures, cancellation and cleanup never exchange identities. Unrelated services receive no application session. Assistant operations retain exactly the intended caller's current authority. Any deployed exposure is assessed separately; an incident is not assumed. |
| **D2 · P1 — I can buy from an installable, upgradeable store** | Seven new business data definitions lack matching checked database/API contracts and accompanying database changes. Physical checkout, managed inventory and refund paths depend on new data capabilities, so not visiting optional pages does not isolate the gap. | A fresh installation and an upgrade from the existing baseline support physical purchases, inventory work, refunds and every enabled advertised capability without missing-contract errors. Existing orders, balances, identities, stock history and in-flight work retain their meaning. The released source, dependencies, API and database contracts agree; a generated runtime bundle alone is insufficient. |
| **D3 · P1 — I can add a purchasable product to my cart** | Product-detail data omits inventory policy required by desktop/mobile purchase controls. Missing policy makes even a positive-stock variant appear unavailable. | The actual detail-to-purchase journey works for managed stock, unmanaged inventory and permitted backorders, with single and multiple variants. Sold-out items remain unavailable. Complete options and eligible prices survive large relations; failed adds restore usable controls without falsely reporting success. |
| **D4 · P1 — My checkout link opens the intended cart safely** | The retained account checkout-link journey omits required caller proof, requests a prohibited customer-ownership change, and attempts to proceed after failure. The newer signed payment-link journey is separate. | Every supported link opens only its intended cart for its authorized recipient. Login/register continuation follows an approved ownership policy. Expired, foreign or failed links show an actionable error and do not advance checkout, transfer another customer's cart or replace a valid cart. |
| **D5 · P1 — My refund/replacement claim has a trustworthy result** | Refund claims submit an unsupported fulfillment state. Purchased-item identity is not established through the existing claim relationship; later item failures can leave partial claims. Direct status changes do not establish money or goods outcomes. | Supported claims retain the exact purchased items and permitted quantities. Failed submissions cannot masquerade as complete claims. Refund/replacement status reflects actual money and goods outcomes; retries cannot duplicate them. Unsupported image evidence stays explicitly unavailable, never a fabricated upload. |
| **D6 · P1 for offered currencies — I see the amount I will actually pay** | The mobile product price still assumes a hundred minor units per currency, despite support elsewhere for currencies with different precision. | Zero-, two- and three-decimal currency examples agree across desktop/mobile product prices, cart, payment, receipt, refund, price authoring and merchant reports. No display silently rescales stored money. Unsupported currencies are declared before purchase. |

## Required acceptance gates for existing journeys

These are unverified release requirements, not additional confirmed defects. Each is **P1** for the affected supported journey.

| ID / user-visible goal | Problem or unresolved assurance | Acceptance criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **R1 — Discover and manage the right product** | Complete, private and usable catalog behavior is not established end-to-end. | Browsing, detail, images, selectable options, regional eligibility, filters, sorting and pagination preserve working baseline behavior. Draft/internal catalog information and private unit costs stay private. Price-list and rule eligibility distinguish anonymous buyers, members and nonmembers, including nested and overlapping prices. Incomplete, oversized or changing reads produce an explicit retry/narrowing error rather than misleading partial results. Merchant variant creation and catalog review remain usable. |
| **R2 — Keep my cart and pay the agreed amount** | Cart recovery, address continuation and monetary agreement need actual customer acceptance. | Guest and registered carts remain isolated. Missing/deleted/expired carts differ from service outages; transient failure does not discard a valid cart or create an unwanted replacement. Saved and distinct billing/shipping addresses work. Quantity pricing, promotions, customer discounts, shipping, tax and gift value reconcile with the accepted amount, including free orders. Later catalog/profile changes do not rewrite accepted prices, items or addresses. |
| **R3 — Pay once and receive one reliable order** | Payment success screens and helper checks do not establish durable financial completion. | Supported Stripe, PayPal, manual-payment and business-account journeys preserve their established authority. Repeated submissions, omitted retry inputs, duplicate/delayed/out-of-order events, interrupted responses, stale recovery work and concurrent purchases cannot duplicate charges, orders, discounts or credit spending. Completion requires matching local financial evidence. Unknown provider outcomes stay visibly unresolved and recoverable; resource failure cannot leave an unacknowledged charge or release an uncertain purchase's stock. Manual tender stays unpaid until actual receipt is recorded. |
| **R4 — Pay an invoice without losing account integrity** | Invoice recovery, account credit and historical receivables need independent acceptance. | Account approval, credit limits, invoice creation/payment and supported credits remain usable under current permissions. Account totals, currency and financial relationships cannot be rewritten outside authorized business outcomes. Refunds, credit notes, paid/unpaid invoices and account credits remain distinguishable; no guessed currency conversion or invented cash refund is permitted. |
| **R5 — Trust my stock and imports** | Stock conservation and truthful bulk-operation results are not established with real concurrent persistence. | Opening stock, adjustments and imports record their actual outcomes, preserve explicit zero quantities and reject invalid/stale input. Duplicate/retried application cannot apply twice, and existing merchant products, prices and stock are not silently overwritten. Competing purchases cannot oversell managed non-backorder stock. Historical reservations remain reconcilable without guessing current policy. |
| **R6 — Receive or return the correct goods and money** | Shipping, physical receipt and financial reversal can diverge; historical behavior remains unverified. | Allocation, label purchase, carrier handoff and delivery remain distinct in customer/operator status. Unknown label purchase or cancellation is not blindly repeated. Partial fulfillment, cancellation, approved unshipped returns, physical receipt and restock/quarantine/discard reflect actual goods movement. Cancellation and returns preserve sale-time stock treatment and original allocations, without double restoration after later policy changes. Refunds cannot exceed remaining accepted entitlement, preserve original tender/currency and distinguish pending, unknown, successful and manually attested outcomes. Historical orders stay actionable without invented financial or stock facts. |
| **R7 — Manage my identity without granting someone else's authority** | Real authentication, machine scopes and relationship protections need boundary-level proof. | Login, registration, profile/password changes, customer credentials, API/OAuth integrations and dashboard access retain intended behavior. Machine authority cannot exceed its permitted scopes and current owner authority; customer tokens do not acquire dashboard powers. Legitimate existing integrations continue to work. Confirmation/payment-link messages reach intended recipients without private credentials, costs or receipt secrets. Queued, delivered, failed and unknown outcomes stay distinct; retry cannot invent delivery success. | **R10 — Finish setup without losing my store** | Fresh/resumed onboarding, regional setup and launch claims need customer-facing acceptance. | Minimal/full/custom setup and interrupted retries retain branding, credentials, edited content, options, prices and stock. ## Optional goals requiring a product decision

These are **P2** unless already promised to a merchant. Existing working equivalents must not be displaced. Each chosen offering must also satisfy the relevant P1 gates above.

| ID / user-visible goal | Conditional acceptance criteria |
| --- | --- |
| **F1 — Use funded gift value** | Issuance states funding evidence, denomination, region, expiration and authority. Balances cannot be overspent. Mixed-tender refunds and failed-checkout release restore the correct value exactly once. Legacy balances without proven denomination remain explicitly unresolved; reconciliation is not currency conversion. Stored-value liability is separate from provider cash and promotional credit. |
| **F2 — Earn and use rewards** | The merchant explicitly chooses eligibility and rate. Customers understand when rewards become available, where they can spend them and what refunds reverse. Spent reward reversals affect future rewards, never an unconsented card charge. Customer/region/currency boundaries hold; pausing awards does not prevent required refund reconciliation. |
| **F3 — Buy private digital products** | Only an entitled purchaser can retrieve the purchased immutable version after sufficient payment. Cancellation, returns and revocation apply according to the stated policy. Private storage/configuration failures are explicit. Physical checkout remains independent of offering digital sales. Digital-only or mixed-product shipping requirements match an explicit supported journey. |
| **F4 — Reorder conveniently** | Invitations require consent, support pause/cancel and a fresh purchase decision at current price, stock, shipping and tax. An unpaid prior invitation does not accumulate unwanted carts. Source-product/customer failures are actionable. No automatic charge, fixed future price, advance stock promise or subscription claim is implied. |
| **F5 — Sell across stock locations** | Allocation and transfer partition, rather than increase, total available stock. Original-location reversals, backorders, receiving decisions and history remain coherent. Aggregate-only merchants are not disrupted. Staff can select and understand locations without relying on manual database inspection. |
| **F6 — Offer live shipping rates** | Offered rates match destination, package, carrier/service, amount, currency and validity. Changed carts invalidate outdated rates; unavailable service is not presented as a usable offer. Label purchase, carrier handoff and return-shipping policy remain distinct. |
| **F7 — Search a larger catalog accurately** | Global counts, facets, regional/customer prices, availability and every sort/page remain correct across the agreed catalog size and concurrent edits. No silent truncation, misleading first-page aggregate or unbounded work is accepted. Current limits remain explicit until supported larger workloads are demonstrated. |
| **F8 — Reconcile settlements and schedule reports** | Exact capture/partial-refund identities match provider exports; duplicates, unmatched rows, fees and payout differences remain visible. Expected payout is not bank receipt or accounting posting. Scheduled retained reports have visible execution and missed-period recovery; enablement alone does not promise delivery or email. |
| **F9 — Resolve customer service work with delegated authority** | Cases retain order identity, assignment, priorities, deadlines and attributable history. Operators can find all supported work and act within actual refund authority. Per-refund ceilings do not imply daily budgets or independent approval. Failed saves and list refreshes remain truthful and recoverable. |
| **F10 — Honor privacy requests and publish truthful policies** | Customers can inspect the stated export scope and preferences; identity-verified requests record actual correction/restriction work and retention decisions. Restrictions hold across declared consumers. A status change is not erasure; publishing policy is not legal certification. Privacy, terms, retention, shipping and return promises match the merchant's actual supported service. |
| **F11 — Add currently unavailable merchant capabilities** | Evidence uploads, custom-region creation, advanced tax, automated subscriptions, disputes, accounting posting, multichannel routing or new Marketplace/OpenShip offerings require an explicit demand/support decision. If offered, the promised outcome actually persists or executes and has appropriate authority and acceptance evidence. Otherwise unavailable states remain honest; historical plans do not create a commitment. |

## P2 investigations before expanding claims

- **Load and capacity:** establish supported catalog/report size, concurrent request behavior, aggregate query work and upload limits. Acceptance: agreed workloads succeed; excess work is rejected without false totals or blocking ordinary customers.
- **Provider and delivery resilience:** establish behavior for malformed/oversized responses, destination changes, outages, delayed callbacks and uncertain external results. Acceptance: no private credential disclosure or duplicate money/goods effects; uncertainty remains attributable and recoverable.
- **Historical and deployed compatibility:** establish actual legacy-data, revoked-authority, in-flight-work and deployment behavior. Acceptance: retained records recover safely and exposure/readiness claims have environment-specific evidence, not inference from local source.
- **Privacy and observation:** establish declared restrictions across consumers, logs, outbound payloads and retained evidence. Acceptance: sensitive information and consent boundaries hold end-to-end; no unsupported security/compliance certification is made.

## Open decisions

1. **Release scope and baseline:** Which deployed merchant and Marketplace versions, workflows and integration contracts must remain compatible? Which optional goals are selected, deferred or already commercially committed?
2. **Assistant exposure:** Is the affected assistant route deployed/used? Who owns containment and historical exposure review, and what evidence would justify session/credential action?
3. **Legacy checkout links:** Are they supported alongside signed payment links? Who may claim or transfer a guest cart after login, and what happens to an existing customer cart?
4. **Claims and replacements:** Keep, converge or explicitly retire the legacy offering? What refund/replacement authority, partial-failure semantics, purchased-item identity, historical-record treatment and evidence storage are promised?
5. **Catalog and pricing privacy:** Which raw amounts, price lists, rules, tags and category metadata are public, member-scoped or staff-only? Eligibility for a discount does not automatically settle visibility of its underlying data.
6. **Money and tax footprint:** Which currencies and tax jurisdictions/policies are supported? How should legacy tax units, gift denomination and missing accepted-price/cost evidence be resolved without guessed conversion or historical rewrites?
7. **Fulfillment meaning:** Does “completed” mean full handoff, delivery or another agreed outcome? How should existing completed orders and ambiguous historical stock reservations be presented and resolved?
8. **Digital, gift and rewards policy:** What payment threshold, access/revocation/return rules, funding evidence, expiration and reward reversal terms are offered? Is shipping-free digital checkout in scope?
9. **Capacity and performance:** What merchant catalog/variant/report sizes and concurrent loads must work? What retry/narrowing experience and freshness guarantees are acceptable beyond current limits?
10. **Operations and delegation:** Who runs and monitors recovery, handles unknown outcomes and owns scheduled work? What refund ceilings, approval expectations, recovery deadlines and retained evidence are required?
11. **Privacy and merchant promises:** Who approves legal/commercial policy, retention, export scope and consent enforcement? Which requests require external/manual work rather than an in-app completion claim?
12. **Recovery of working material:** Is the owner preserving the local fixes, tests and audit evidence, or deliberately accepting their loss? The requirements document is not a source/evidence backup and is itself untracked. Acceptance before any discard: a separately saved, readable copy of these requirements exists outside the cleanup scope; any desired source, tests and historical evidence are independently preserved, and loss or reimplementation of the remainder is explicitly accepted.

## Release acceptance criteria

Confirmed problems are addressed first; affected existing journeys satisfy their P1 gates; optional goals are explicitly selected or deferred. New acceptance evidence identifies the exact proposed release and actual deployed integration baselines. Historical pass counts, test presence, staged status, a generated bundle or isolated helper checks do not establish application, database, provider or deployed readiness.
